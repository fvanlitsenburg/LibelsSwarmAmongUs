RESEARCH QUESTION
Does this pamphlet provide evidence relevant to Anglo-Dutch political
relations, propaganda, or information warfare in the period surrounding
the Rampjaar (roughly 1650–1680), including the mechanics, content, or
effects of pamphlet campaigns aimed at shaping domestic or foreign
political opinion?

RELEVANT WHEN
- The pamphlet concerns Anglo-Dutch diplomatic, military, or trade
  relations (war, treaties — secret or public, negotiations, naval
  incidents, the Navigation Acts, fishery disputes).
- The pamphlet concerns the House of Orange / stadtholderate question,
  especially where it intersects with English politics or Anglo-Dutch
  relations (e.g. William III's minority, States of Holland withholding
  Orange offices, English pressure on the stadtholderate).
- The pamphlet is itself an act of domestic political persuasion or
  propaganda — a "praetje," dialogue, satire, examination, or polemic
  aimed at shaping public or parliamentary opinion — covering national domestic issues
- The pamphlet covers war engagements, specifically Anglo-Dutch or from the 1672 Rampjaar
- The pamphlet covers questions of foreign affairs with a bearing on English or Dutch politics (or both)
- The pamphlet involves legal or procedural material (trials,
  examinations, interrogations) tied to a political crisis with an
  Anglo-Dutch or Orangist dimension.
  - The pamphlet involves a religious question that affects relations between European states (for example, fear of Catholicism in England vis-a-vis James II)

IRRELEVANT WHEN
- The pamphlet is purely religious/theological with no discernible
  political dimension connecting to England or the Orange question.
- The pamphlet is purely local/municipal administrative material
  (guild disputes, parish records, etc.) unconnected to national
  politics or Anglo-Dutch relations.
- The pamphlet concerns foreign affairs entirely unrelated to England
  or the Orange question (e.g. Baltic trade disputes, unrelated
  colonial matters).
- The pamphlet falls well outside the c. 1650–1680 window and has no
  explicit bearing on that period as precursor or aftermath.

IMPORTANT CONSERVATIVE RULES

  actual subject.
- Poor OCR should lead to UNCERTAIN, not STOP.
